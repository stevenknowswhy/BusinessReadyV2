# Starting a business in San Francisco: SaaS vs. Retail vs. Nonprofit

What it takes to *just start* — legally operating, not yet funding-ready. Figures verified September 2026. This is orientation, not legal advice; the app should always point users to verify with the cited agencies.

## Part 1: The outline

### The common spine (all three)

1. **Choose and file your entity** with the CA Secretary of State — LLC $70, corporation $100, filed online via bizfile.
2. **EIN** from the IRS (free, ~15 minutes online).
3. **CA $800/year franchise tax** — owed by LLCs and corps every year including year one (the first-year exemption expired after 2023). Due the 15th day of the 4th month after formation, then every April 15.
4. **Statement of Information** — $20, within 90 days of formation, then every two years.
5. **SF Business Registration** with the Office of the Treasurer & Tax Collector — required for anyone "engaging in business" in SF (shop, office, soliciting, or working 7+ days/year). Annual: valid July 1–June 30, renew by May 31. Fees $75+ based on gross receipts (Prop E gross receipts tax).
6. **Fictitious Business Name** with the SF County Clerk if operating under a trade name.
7. **Business bank account + bookkeeping** from day one.

### SaaS extras

- **Entity decision:** investor standard is a Delaware C-corp, but operating in SF still triggers CA registration and the $800 tax — you don't escape California by incorporating elsewhere.
- **CCPA/CPRA privacy compliance:** privacy policy, data inventory, opt-out mechanism if handling Californians' personal data.
- **Terms of service.**
- Generally **no** seller's permit (pure SaaS isn't tangible goods), **no** storefront or Planning permits.

### Brick-and-mortar retail extras (the niche — the compliance maze)

- **Seller's permit** from the CDTFA — required to sell tangible goods; you collect and remit sales tax.
- **SF Planning:** zoning verification and change-of-use permits for the space; **formula retail conditional use** review for chains in covered districts.
- **Commercial lease** review before signing.
- **Sign permits** under the SF sign code.
- **Food:** SF Department of Public Health permits + inspections. **Tobacco/alcohol:** additional state and local licenses.
- **Employer stack:** EDD registration, workers' comp, SF minimum wage **$19.61/hr** (as of July 1, 2026), paid sick leave, the SF Health Care Security Ordinance (employer health spending), required workplace postings.
- **ADA** physical access for the space.
- **Business personal property statement** to the Assessor (fixtures, inventory) — due April 1.

### Nonprofit extras

- **CA nonprofit public benefit corporation** articles (with the required charitable clauses), filed with the Secretary of State.
- **Bylaws, board of directors** (3+ typical for 501(c)(3)), conflict-of-interest policy.
- **IRS Form 1023 / 1023-EZ** for 501(c)(3) status.
- **CA FTB exemption** (Form 3500) — separate from the IRS, and you owe the $800 until it's granted.
- **CA Attorney General Registry of Charities** (Form CT-1) — required before soliciting donations in California.
- Selling goods (thrift shop, merch)? **Seller's permit still required.**
- SF business registration still required; verify nonprofit fee treatment with TTX.

## Why retail is the niche

Retail has roughly double the compliance surface of SaaS: state tax permits, city planning, signage, health, labor, and property filings — most of them SF-specific, most of them *blocking* (you cannot open the doors without them). Miss one and the penalty is immediate and visible: fines, closure, no opening day. That density is the wedge — it's exactly the kind of maze a guided, proactive app earns its keep navigating. SaaS founders can Google their way through; a first-time SF shop owner cannot.
